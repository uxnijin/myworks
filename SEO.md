# SEO: what is done, and what only you can do

The technical half is in the repo and deploys with it. The other half is
off-site, needs your accounts and your name on it, and is listed here in the
order that pays back fastest.

Nothing in this file is a guarantee about ranking. Anyone who gives you one is
selling something: Google does not publish the algorithm, and a new site does
not outrank an established one on a competitive phrase in a fortnight, however
good the markup is. What is true is that this site was, until now, invisible to
half the crawlers that matter, and that is fixed.

---

## Done, in the repo

- **Every route is a real HTML file.** 59 of them, generated by
  `tools/prerender.js`, each with its own title, description, canonical, social
  tags, JSON-LD, and the article's full text. Previously every URL served one
  untitled shell with an empty `<div>`. See CLAUDE.md, "The site is prerendered".
- **One head, decided in one place** — `SEO` in `data.js`, baked into the static
  page and re-applied by `applySeo()` when the router navigates.
- **Structured data**: `Person`, `WebSite`, `ProfilePage`, `CollectionPage`,
  `BreadcrumbList`, `Article` on every case study and finding,
  `SoftwareApplication` on every product, and `ProfessionalService` + `FAQPage`
  on `/hire`. All of it one connected `@graph` pointing at a single `@id` for
  you, so the site describes one entity rather than fifty unrelated authors.
- **`/hire`** — the only page written for someone deciding rather than browsing,
  and the only one that answers "hire a ui ux designer", "freelance product
  designer", "app design services". It is in the top nav, the drawer and the
  footer.
- **Social cards** — 1200x630 PNGs per entry, built from the entry's own
  screenshot by `tools/make_og.py`.
- **`sitemap.xml`** generated from the route list, with 143 figures declared for
  Google Images. **`llms.txt`** likewise. **`robots.txt`** blocks nothing and
  names the answer engines explicitly.
- **Speed**: scripts deferred from the head, the marquee's off-screen frames
  left lazy instead of loading sixty-two at once, and an LCP preload hint on
  every document.

## 1. Tell Google the site exists — 20 minutes, do this first

Nothing below matters until this is done, and none of it can be done for you.

1. **Google Search Console** — <https://search.google.com/search-console>. Add
   `nijin.site` as a **Domain** property (not a URL prefix), which needs one DNS
   TXT record at your registrar and covers every subdomain in one go.
2. Submit `https://nijin.site/sitemap.xml` under **Sitemaps**.
3. Use **URL Inspection → Request indexing** on `/`, `/hire`, `/designs`,
   `/case-studies` and `/products`. Five is enough; the sitemap carries the rest.
4. **Bing Webmaster Tools** — <https://www.bing.com/webmasters>. It imports
   straight from Search Console, takes two minutes, and it is what ChatGPT's
   search index is built on. Worth more in 2026 than its market share suggests.
5. Come back in a week and read **Pages → Why pages aren't indexed**. That
   report is the only honest source of truth about what is actually happening.

## 2. Turn the analytics on — 5 minutes

`ANALYTICS.clarityProjectId` in `analytics.js` is **empty**, so `window.track`
is a stub and nothing at all is being recorded. Every wiring around it is
already written: per-route page views, custom tags per page, named events for
the clicks that matter.

Get the ID from <https://clarity.microsoft.com> → Settings → Overview, paste it
in, commit. Until then there is no way to tell whether any of this worked.

## 3. Your own tools are your best backlinks, and they are underused

You have six live tools on subdomains of nijin.site with roughly **22,000 users
between them**. Five link back to the portfolio. This is what they say:

| Tool | Users | Links back | Anchor text |
|---|---|---|---|
| speedtest.nijin.site | 11,200 | yes | "Nijin" |
| sitespeedcheck.nijin.site | 6,300 | yes | "Nijin" |
| dnschecker.nijin.site | 2,700 | yes | "Nijin" |
| **ipchecker.nijin.site** | **1,900** | **no** | — |
| design.nijin.site | 500 | yes | "Nijin" |
| icons.nijin.site | — | yes | "Nijin" |
| tintsandshades.nijin.site | — | yes | "Portfolio" |

Two things to fix, in the repos those tools live in:

- **IP Checker has no link home at all.** Add the same credit line the others
  carry. It is the single cheapest thing on this list.
- **"Nijin" is a weak anchor.** Anchor text is one of the clearer signals you
  control outright. Change the credit to something like:

  > Built by [Nijin Muhammed, a UI/UX and product designer](https://nijin.site/).

  Do not make all six identical, and do not stuff them — vary the wording, and
  point one or two at `/hire` rather than the root.

## 4. Claim the profiles that link back

Every one of these gets a link and, more importantly, an entity Google can tie
to yours through the `sameAs` list already in the site's schema. Use the exact
same name, role and URL on all of them — consistency is the point.

**Already in your schema**: Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube, GitHub, Figma, Medium.
Make sure each of those profiles actually carries the `nijin.site` link in its
website field; a `sameAs` that is not reciprocated does much less work.

**Worth adding**, roughly in order of value for a designer:

- **Behance** and **Dribbble** — the two the industry checks. Post the case
  studies as projects with a link back to the full write-up here.
- **Google Business Profile** — free, and the strongest single lever on
  "ui ux designer in Kozhikode" or "product designer Kerala". You need a
  service-area listing rather than a shopfront.
- **Read.cv / Polywork / Contra / Wellfound** — designer-facing profiles that
  hiring managers genuinely browse.
- **Product Hunt** — you have shipped seven Figma plugins and two Chrome
  extensions. Each is a legitimate launch, and each launch is a link.
- **Figma Community** — your plugin pages should point at the case study here,
  not just at the plugin.
- **Chrome Web Store** — both extensions are currently **Unlisted**, which means
  nobody can find them and neither gives you a link. Publishing them is worth
  more than any directory on this list.
- **Awwwards / CSSDA / Siteinspire** — submissions, not profiles. Long odds, but
  a listing is a strong link.

One caveat worth knowing: most profile links are `nofollow`. They still work —
they establish the entity, they carry real people to the site, and Google has
treated `nofollow` as a hint rather than an instruction since 2019. What does
not work is buying links, or a hundred directory submissions in a weekend.
That pattern is the one thing Google is genuinely good at catching.

## 5. Write the things that earn links on their own

You already have the rarest asset in this market: **real running builds, shot
as real screenshots**. Most portfolios are mockups. Lean on it.

- **`/findings` is the link magnet, not the portfolio.** Nobody links to
  someone else's case study, but people link to "Bring undo to more web
  interactions" all the time. Sixteen findings is a good start; it is also the
  cheapest content on the site to add to.
- **Cross-post with a canonical.** Medium and dev.to both let you set a
  canonical URL back to the original. Post findings there; the traffic finds
  you and the ranking stays here.
- **Answer where designers ask.** Reddit r/UI_Design, r/userexperience,
  Designer Hangout, local Kerala tech communities. Not link-drops — answers,
  with a link where it is genuinely the answer.
- **The tools are stories.** "I built a speed test that 11,000 people use" is a
  post. So is "what I learned shipping seven Figma plugins".

## 6. What to watch, and when

Do not check rankings daily. They move on their own and you will read noise as
signal.

- **Week 1**: Search Console shows pages discovered and indexed. That is the
  only number that matters at this stage.
- **Week 2-4**: impressions start appearing for brand terms ("nijin", "nijin
  muhammed") and long-tail phrases.
- **Month 2-3**: movement on the middle-difficulty phrases — "ui ux designer
  kerala", "figma plugin for cmyk export", the case study names.
- **Month 4+**: the broad head terms — "ui designer", "product designer" — if
  they ever move at all. They are contested by agencies with a decade of links
  and a budget. Ranking for "ui ux designer in Kozhikode" and for the specific
  problems you have written about is worth far more traffic that actually
  converts, and it is achievable.

Re-run `node tools/prerender.js --check` in CI or before a deploy if you want to
be sure the generated pages never go stale.
